In this section we examine the topic of VAT in the ZEV. The decision as to whether and how VAT must be shown and also paid to the government is fairly complex.

In this section we want to work through the initial questions and the decisions derived from them.

## How does VAT work?

Value added tax is first and foremost a turnover tax.

A company that is liable for VAT must levy a tax on its turnover (sale of products and services), collect it and then pay it to the government.

At the same time, however, companies liable for VAT have a right to deduct input VAT on the services they purchase.

The aim is to tax the VAT on a product only once in total, according to its value at the end consumer.

![VAT ZEV / Ancillary Costs – Figure 1](/img/konfiguration-billing-mwst-zev-nebenkosten/01.png)

Details of the example

So if a company A orders energy of 100 kWh for CHF 100 net from the supplier, it receives from the VAT-liable utility an invoice for the 100 kWh of energy (CHF 100 net) + the VAT (8.1%, CHF 8.10) of CHF 108.10.

Company A pays CHF 108.10, the utility pays the CHF 8.10 to the government and retains CHF 100 for its services.

If company A were now to resell this energy to a consumer (here without profit, for better understanding), company A again offers the consumer the 100 kWh of energy at a price of CHF 100 and again levies the VAT itself, since company A is itself liable for VAT.

The invoice to the consumer therefore looks as follows:

100 kWh of energy (CHF 100 net) + the VAT (8.1%, CHF 8.10) of CHF 108.10.

The consumer pays the CHF 108.10 to company A.

Company A receives CHF 100 for the energy and pays the VAT of CHF 8.10 to the government.

Now the important point:
On the amounts of VAT to be paid, company A may now claim the input tax deduction before settling the debt.

From the input tax deduction, company A may deduct CHF 8.10 (previously paid to the utility in the energy invoice).
Since there was no mark-up on the energy price (no profit), the debt from the sales amounts to CHF 8.10 - the input tax deduction = CHF 0
Company A therefore does not have to pay anything additional; for company A, CHF 100 was spent and CHF 100 was received.

The government has had the CHF 8.10 in its account for the energy from the outset, and the consumer, in this case not liable for VAT themselves and therefore not entitled to a deduction, cannot reclaim the CHF 8.10. Thus only the end consumer pays the VAT on the product or the service.

Everyone in between can reclaim the VAT in each case.

## When is one liable for VAT and for what? (as of 01.2026)

In a ZEV / vZEV, primarily one kind of product and one service are sold.
In this case the product is electrical energy and the service is the billing of this energy.

Increasingly, however, heat / water and ancillary costs are also billed together with the ZEV.
In this context there are various additional constellations which must be considered for VAT.

For each form of energy it must be decided individually whether a VAT liability applies to it or not.

### Is the ZEV liable for VAT on electrical energy and services?

![VAT ZEV / Ancillary Costs – Figure 2](/img/konfiguration-billing-mwst-zev-nebenkosten/02.jpg)

A VAT liability can in principle arise through the following conditions:

1.  Turnover from energy and service sales of the ZEV > CHF 100,000 / year --> automatic tax liability

2.  Through a voluntary subjection of the ZEV to VAT liability (opting of the ZEV)


In both cases this must be registered with the tax office and a turnover tax number must be obtained for the ZEV.

If one of the two applies, the following will happen with regard to the ZEV:

The ZEV is liable for VAT:

1.  The ZEV levies VAT on all electricity sales (grid energy and solar and battery electricity) and shows it on the invoices.

2.  The ZEV is entitled to deduct input VAT for purchases and services in connection with electrical energy.

3.  The ZEV must settle the VAT with the government after the input tax deduction


The ZEV is not liable for VAT:

1.  The energy prices are billed at gross prices without VAT information

2.  The services and energy-related ancillary costs are billed at gross prices without VAT information


### Is there a VAT liability on heat / water and ancillary costs?

This question can only be answered through the way these forms of energy are sold and the relationship and mandate with regard to the property + the questions on turnover and opting.

Case 1 (details see below): Are the forms of energy produced in the property or purchased by the landlord and then distributed?
\--> central heating, district heating and allocation through ancillary costs to the occupants

Case 2 (details see below): Are the forms of energy produced and delivered directly from the ZEV to the tenant?
\--> utility situation, e.g. district heating?

Measured against these two examples, it is clearly apparent that the relationships and references are different and can accordingly lead to different results.

Case 1:

![VAT ZEV / Ancillary Costs – Figure 3](/img/konfiguration-billing-mwst-zev-nebenkosten/03.jpg)

Details:

The forms of energy are generated in the property by a heat pump with a domestic hot water tank and then distributed within the properties.
or
The case of the condominium owners' association which purchases energy and then distributes it.

Since the forms of energy were generated in the property by equipment owned by the property owner, the question now arises whether the property owner is liable for VAT. (energy and ancillary costs)

The ZEV or billing service provider bills these forms of energy as a service to the property owner under mandate. The ZEV or billing service provider must levy VAT for the billing service itself, provided it is liable for VAT, but not for the energy and ancillary costs to be allocated.

The property owner can in principle be liable for VAT themselves for the same reasons:

1.  Turnover from taxable services > CHF 100,000 / year --> automatic tax liability
    Taxable services are, for example, the rental of holiday apartments or parking spaces without reference to apartment rentals.
    Services that are provided in-house are also taxable services.
    Normal apartment rents themselves are not taxable services.

2.  Through a voluntary subjection of the property to VAT liability (opting)


The most common option is voluntary subjection. A liability arising from turnover occurs rather rarely, since the rental of purely residential space is not a taxable service.

Opting (partial opting) of a property

Opting for a property is to be decided by the owner of the property. It can be a clever move for the rental of commercial space, since the businesses can then claim the VAT as an input tax deduction.

If the decision to opt is taken, the entire property owned by the same landlord is opted and voluntarily made subject to VAT.

This is important to understand, because it now places all parts of the property belonging to this landlord under VAT. An exception under the law is purely residential space, which cannot be opted.

Example:

If the building with 4 units (2x commercial space, 2x residential space) is entirely owned by owner A and this owner opts, then they are effectively partially opted for all parts of the property which are not purely residential space.

1.  Commercial space 1 --> opted

2.  Commercial space 2 --> opted

3.  Residential space 1 --> not opted, because residential space is exempt from VAT

4.  Residential space 2 --> not opted, because residential space is exempt from VAT


For all opted units the following must now happen:

- Show heat / water invoice amounts net incl. VAT.
    What is important to note here is that the VAT aligns with the VAT rate of the rents for all components.
    Expenses with a reduced tax rate (2.6% for cold water) must subsequently also be taxed at the standard rate of 8.1%.

- Show ancillary costs invoice amounts net incl. VAT.

- Show rent net incl. VAT! (with the opting of the property, the rent is also placed under VAT liability)


For all units not opted (residential spaces):

- Heat / water invoice amounts at gross prices without VAT being shown.

- Ancillary costs invoice amounts at gross prices without VAT being shown.


In addition to the tax liability, the property owner is now also entitled to deduct input VAT for all services which concern the opted parts of the property, but not for those not opted.

If the ZEV or billing service provider acts under mandate as a service provider for the heat / water and ancillary costs, the following is important:

- It must be clearly evident on the invoice to the tenant that the invoice was created on behalf of the landlord.
    If this is not clearly expressed, the sender of the invoice is deemed to be the issuer of the invoice. Since the invoices for rent and ancillary costs would then not come from the same issuer, the entitlement to VAT exemption for the heat / water and ancillary costs lapses, as they are no longer regarded as part of the rent!

    A note text: Invoicing and dispatch on behalf of your landlord XXXXXXXXXX
    If the landlord is liable for tax, with the landlord's VAT number stated.
