In this section, we take a closer look at VAT in the ZEV (association for own consumption). Deciding whether and how VAT must be shown on invoices and paid to the state is fairly complex. 

In this section, we want to retrace the initial questions and the decisions derived from them.

## How does VAT work?

Value added tax is primarily a sales tax. 

A company that is liable for VAT must levy a tax on its turnover (sale of products and services), collect it and then pay it to the state.

At the same time, however, companies liable for VAT have the right to deduct input tax on purchased goods and services.

The goal is for VAT on a product to be taxed only once in total, corresponding to its value to the end consumer.

![VAT ZEV / ancillary costs – Figure 1](/img/konfiguration-billing-mwst-zev-nebenkosten/01.png)

Details of the example

If a company A orders 100 kWh of energy for CHF 100 net from the supplier, it receives an invoice from the VAT-liable energy supplier for the 100 kWh of energy (CHF 100 net) + VAT (8.1%, CHF 8.10), totalling CHF 108.10.

Company A pays CHF 108.10; the energy supplier pays the CHF 8.10 to the state and keeps CHF 100 for its services.

If company A now resold this energy to a consumer (here without profit, for better understanding), company A again offers the consumer the 100 kWh of energy at a price of CHF 100 and itself levies VAT, since company A is itself liable for VAT. 

The invoice to the consumer is therefore as follows:

100 kWh of energy (CHF 100 net) + VAT (8.1%, CHF 8.10), totalling CHF 108.10.

The consumer pays the CHF 108.10 to company A.

Company A receives CHF 100 for the energy and pays the VAT of CHF 8.10 to the state.

Now the important point:
On the VAT amounts to be paid, company A may now claim the input tax deduction before settling its liability.

From the input tax deduction, company A may deduct CHF 8.10 (previously paid to the energy supplier in the energy invoice).
Since there was no mark-up on the energy price (no profit), the liability from sales amounts to CHF 8.10 - the input tax deduction = CHF 0
Company A therefore does not have to pay anything extra; company A spent CHF 100 and took in CHF 100.

The state has had the CHF 8.10 for the energy in its account from the start, and the consumer, who in this case is not liable for VAT and therefore cannot deduct it, cannot reclaim the CHF 8.10. As a result, only the end consumer pays the VAT on the product or service.

Everyone in between can reclaim the VAT in each case.

## When are you liable for VAT, and for what? (As of 01.2026)

In a ZEV (association for own consumption) / vZEV (virtual ZEV), primarily one kind of product and one service are sold.
In this case, the product is electrical energy and the service is the billing of this energy.

Increasingly, however, heat / water and ancillary costs are also charged together with the ZEV.
In this context, there are various additional constellations that must be considered with regard to VAT.

For each energy, it must be decided individually whether VAT liability applies or not.

### Is the ZEV liable for VAT on electrical energy and services?

![VAT ZEV / ancillary costs – Figure 2](/img/konfiguration-billing-mwst-zev-nebenkosten/02.jpg)

VAT liability can in principle arise from the following conditions:

1.  Turnover from energy and service sales of the ZEV (association for own consumption) > CHF 100'000 / year --> Automatic tax liability

2.  Through voluntary registration of the ZEV for VAT liability (opting of the ZEV)


In both cases, this must be registered with the tax office and a VAT number must be obtained for the ZEV.

If either of the two applies, the following will happen with regard to the ZEV:

The ZEV is liable for VAT:

1.  The ZEV levies VAT on all electricity sales (grid energy as well as solar and battery electricity) and shows it on the invoices.

2.  The ZEV is entitled to deduct input tax on purchases and services related to electrical energy.

3.  The ZEV must pay the VAT to the state after the input tax deduction


The ZEV is not liable for VAT:

1.  Energy prices are charged at gross prices without VAT information

2.  Services and ancillary energy costs are charged at gross prices without VAT information


### Is there VAT liability on heat / water and ancillary costs?

This question can only be answered based on the way these energies are sold and the relationship and mandate with regard to the property + the questions on turnover and opting.

Case 1 (details see below): Are the energies produced in the property or purchased by the landlord and then distributed?
\--> Central heating, district heating and allocation to residents via ancillary costs

Case 2 (details see below): Are the energies produced and delivered directly by the ZEV (association for own consumption) to the tenant?
\--> Energy supplier situation, e.g. district heating?

Based on these two examples, it is clear that the relationships and references differ and can accordingly lead to different results.

Case 1:

![VAT ZEV / ancillary costs – Figure 3](/img/konfiguration-billing-mwst-zev-nebenkosten/03.jpg)

Details:

The energies are generated in the property by a heat pump with a hot water storage tank and then distributed within the properties.
or
The case of the condominium owners' association (STWEG), which purchases energy and then distributes it.

Since the energies in the property were generated by equipment owned by the property owner, the question now arises whether the property owner is liable for VAT. (Energies and ancillary costs)

The ZEV or billing service provider bills these energies as a service to the property owner under a mandate. The ZEV or billing service provider must levy VAT on the billing service itself, provided it is liable for VAT, but not on the energies and ancillary costs to be allocated.

The property owner can in principle be liable for VAT for the same reasons:

1.  Turnover from taxable supplies  > CHF 100'000 / year --> Automatic tax liability
    Taxable supplies are, for example, the rental of holiday apartments or parking spaces not related to apartment rentals.
    Services provided by the owner themselves are also taxable supplies.
    Normal apartment rents themselves are not taxable supplies.

2.  Through voluntary registration of the property for VAT liability (opting)


The most common option is voluntary registration. Liability due to turnover is rather rare, since renting purely residential space is not a taxable supply.

Opting (partial opting) of a property

The decision to opt a property is made by the owner of the property. It can be a smart move when renting commercial space, since the businesses can then deduct the VAT as input tax.

If the decision to opt is made, the entire property owned by the same landlord is opted and voluntarily made subject to VAT.

This is important to understand, because this now places all parts of the property belonging to this landlord under VAT. By law, the exception is purely residential space, which cannot be opted.

Example:

If the building with 4 units (2x commercial space, 2x residential space) is entirely owned by owner A and owner A opts, then owner A is effectively partially opted for all parts of the property that are not purely residential space.

1.  Commercial space 1 --> opted

2.  Commercial space 2 --> opted

3.  Residential space 1 --> not opted, because residential space is exempt from VAT

4.  Residential space 2 --> not opted, because residential space is exempt from VAT


For all opted units, the following must now happen:

- Show heat / water invoice amounts net incl. VAT.
    It is important to note here that the VAT is aligned with the VAT rate of the rents for all components.
    Expenses with a reduced tax rate (2.6% for cold water) must also subsequently be taxed at the standard rate of 8.1%.

- Show ancillary cost invoice amounts net incl. VAT.

- Show rent net incl. VAT! (When the property is opted, the rent also becomes subject to VAT)


For all non-opted units (residential spaces):

- Heat / water invoice amounts at gross prices without showing VAT.

- Ancillary cost invoice amounts at gross prices without showing VAT.


In addition to the tax liability, however, the property owner is now also entitled to deduct input tax on all supplies relating to the opted parts of the property, but not for the non-opted ones.

If the ZEV or billing service provider acts under a mandate as the service provider for heat / water and ancillary costs, the following is important:

- The invoice to the tenant must clearly show that the invoice was issued on behalf of the landlord.
    If this is not clearly stated, the sender of the invoice is deemed to be the invoicing party. Since the invoices for rent and ancillary costs would then not come from the same invoicing party, the entitlement to VAT exemption for heat / water and ancillary costs lapses, as they are no longer regarded as part of the rent!

    A note text: Invoicing and dispatch on behalf of your landlord XXXXXXXXXX
    If the landlord is liable for tax, with the landlord's VAT number.
