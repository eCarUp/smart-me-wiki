## Contact for questions

### Self-consumption rate and self-sufficiency rate

![Guide for tenants – figure 13](/img/nutzeranleitungen-mieter/13.jpg)

The self-consumption rate and self-sufficiency rate of a building are shown. The values are calculated from the PV meter and the balance meter.

Self-consumption rate: How much of the solar energy is consumed directly in the building.

Self-sufficiency rate: How much of the total consumption is produced by the solar system.



### Consumption and production monitoring

![Guide for tenants – figure 14](/img/nutzeranleitungen-mieter/14.jpg)

Shows where the energy produced is consumed (in the building or fed into the grid) as well as where the energy consumed was drawn from (solar or from the grid).  

### Meter types

In the app, each meter can be viewed individually.

Meters of an apartment

- Electricity meter (Elektro 470P2 WHG 11...)
    Measures the electricity consumed (kWh)

- Heating meter (Heizung WHG 11.4...)
    Measures the energy consumed to operate the heating system (kWh)

- Cold water meter (Kaltwasser WHG 11.4...)
    Measures the cold water consumption (m3)

- Domestic hot water meter (Warmwasser WHG 11.4...)
    Measures the domestic hot water consumption (m3)




Billing-relevant meters of a ZEV

- Photovoltaic system (Elekto 32P2 PVA)
    Required to measure the energy produced by the PV system.

- Balance meter (Elekto 26P4 Bilanzm...)
    Required to know how much electricity was drawn from the grid or fed into it.

- Total consumption (Verbrauch Feld 37)
    Required to determine the total consumption of the property/apartment (PV and grid consumption).

## How do I read a VEWA billing statement correctly?

Billing according to VEWA (consumption-based billing of energy and water costs) governs how the costs for heat and domestic hot water in an apartment building are distributed among the tenants. The aim is to allocate the costs fairly and transparently, in order to take actual consumption into account and create incentives to save.

In a first step, the landlord determines the actual costs incurred for heat and domestic hot water production during the billing period. Since these are based on real consumption and operating costs, the final amounts are only known at the end of the billing period.

These costs are then divided into two categories: basic costs and consumption-based costs.

- Basic costs comprise fixed costs such as servicing and maintenance of the heating and domestic hot water systems. They are based on the total area of the building (m2) and are distributed proportionally according to the apartment size (m2).

- Consumption costs reflect the actual energy consumption of the tenants and are calculated according to the meter values or other measurement methods.


Meters ensure that each tenant pays only for the energy costs they have actually caused. This promotes a fair distribution of costs and encourages a more conscious use of energy.

VEWA billing is standardized, which ensures a transparent and uniform distribution of costs. This makes it easier for tenants to understand the billing statement and to compare it with other apartments. Clear rules for recording and charging the costs additionally help to avoid misunderstandings or disputes.

### Determining the real costs (landlord side)

In our example, the landlord recorded total costs of 2000.- CHF at the end of the billing period. 1000.- CHF each for heat generation (1) and 1000.- CHF for domestic hot water generation (2).

The heat generated is reported in kWh and the domestic hot water in m3. 

To bring the 47.01 m3 of domestic hot water to the desired temperature (e.g. 60°C), 2870.31 kWh were required.

The total costs of 2000.- CHF are therefore divided according to the energy expended in each case (kWh):

Heat (4522 kWh) 1223.43 CHF (3)
Domestic hot water (47.01 m3 or 2870.31 kWh)   776.57 CHF (4)

### Distribution of the costs among tenants

For the split of the total costs into basic costs (30%) and consumption costs (70%), this example uses the distribution key customary in the industry.

### Heat example

In the previous step we determined a total amount of 1223.43 CHF for the heat of the property. This is now split proportionally into 30% basic costs and 70% consumption costs for the entire property. This corresponds to the following costs:

- Basic costs heat 367.03 CHF (5)

- Consumption costs heat 856.40 CHF (6)


In order to distribute these costs fairly among the tenants, the basic costs are allocated on the basis of the living area and the consumption costs (kWh) on the basis of the measured consumption.

The entire property has a living area of 250m2 and our apartment has a living area of 150m2. The basic costs are therefore charged to the apartment at 60% ((100/250)x150).

- Basic costs heat: 367.03 CHF x 60% = 220.22 CHF (7)


![Guide for tenants – figure 19](/img/nutzeranleitungen-mieter/19.png)

An energy consumption of 4522 kWh was measured for the entire property. Our apartment has a separate electricity meter which measured a consumption of 2450 kWh. The consumption costs are therefore charged to the apartment at 54.18% ((100/4522)x2450).

Consumption costs heat: 856.40 CHF x 54.18% = 464.- CHF (8)

The total costs\* for the tenants of the apartment for heat therefore amount to 220.22 CHF + 464.- CHF = 684.21 CHF (9).

### Domestic hot water example

The calculation for the distribution of domestic hot water is done in the same way as the calculation for heat. In the previous step we determined a total amount of 776.57 CHF for the domestic hot water of the property. This is now split proportionally into 30% basic costs and 70% consumption costs for the entire property. This corresponds to the following costs:

- Basic costs domestic hot water 232.97 CHF (10)

- Consumption costs domestic hot water 543.60 CHF (11)


In order to distribute these costs fairly among the tenants, the basic costs are allocated on the basis of the living area and the consumption costs on the basis of the measured consumption (m3).

The entire property has a living area of 250m2 and our apartment has a living area of 150m2. The basic costs are therefore charged to the apartment at 60% ((100/250)x150).

- Basic costs domestic hot water: 232.97 CHF x 60% = 139.78 CHF (12)


A domestic hot water consumption of 47.01 m3 was measured for the entire property. Our apartment has a separate domestic hot water meter which measured a consumption of 26.68 m3. The consumption costs are therefore charged to the apartment at 56.75% ((100/47.01)x26.68).

Consumption costs domestic hot water: 543.60 CHF x 56.75% = 308.51 CHF (13)

The total costs\* for the tenants of the apartment for domestic hot water therefore amount to 139.78 CHF + 308.51 CHF = 448.29 CHF (14)

\* The exact values, not the rounded values displayed, were used to determine the total costs.
