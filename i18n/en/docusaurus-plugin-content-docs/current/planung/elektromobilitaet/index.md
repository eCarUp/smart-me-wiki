## Planning e-mobility

E-mobility is a major consumer within a property or site. As the number of available charging stations grows, the following points must be clarified for every planned infrastructure:

### Load management for e-mobility

Load management, or the behaviour of the charging stations with defined resources, is directly related to the actual availability of the charging stations and the reliability of the entire installation.

As a general rule, a load management system must actively control capacity distribution as soon as there are two or more charging stations in the system.

This is to prevent excessive power consumption by the charging infrastructure from tripping the protective devices that safeguard the infrastructure.
The result would be a temporary power outage in parts of the site or across the entire site, as well as service work to restore normal operation.

At the same time, a reduction in charging power must be ensured if the energy supplier has to protect its grid in the event of undersupply.

In addition to overload protection, load management usually performs further functions:

- Overload protection of the installation

- Optimisation of energy distribution across active charging sessions within the site

- Consideration of other consumers in the system and prioritisation of these consumers over the charging infrastructure

- Prioritisation of the charging groups among each other

- Prioritisation of tariff usage for charging the vehicles (e.g. grid-power or solar-optimised)


You can find more details under "Load management description" in the respective topic section.

### Billing e-mobility to tenants

Charging stations are large consumers and generate corresponding costs when used.
Primarily the energy used for charging, but also the operating power and the resulting losses, as well as any rental fee for the charging station.

Billing can be carried out at a single tariff or multiple tariffs, e.g. peak, off-peak and solar tariff, depending on the hardware used and the combination of billing solutions.

You can find more details under "Billing" in the respective topic section.

### Billing e-mobility to third parties or unknown users (public / semi-public)

Installing charging infrastructure often involves high costs. A good way to speed up amortisation, or to enable a desired public offering, is to make the charging station accessible to third parties. 

To make a charging station available to the public, an e-mobility backend software is required.

The backend software then handles the authorisation of a charging session for third parties, depending on your requirements and the selected payment method.

You can find more details under "Billing" in the respective topic section.
