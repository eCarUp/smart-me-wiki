## Planning e-mobility

E-mobility is a major consumer within a property or site. As the number of available charging stations increases, the following points must be clarified for every planned infrastructure:

### Load management for e-mobility

Load management, or how the charging stations behave with defined resources, is directly related to the actual availability of the charging stations and the reliability of the entire installation.

As a general rule, load management must actively control the capacity distribution as soon as there are two or more charging stations in the system.

This is intended to prevent excessive power consumption by the charging infrastructure from tripping the fuses that protect the infrastructure.
This would result in a temporary power outage in parts of the site or the entire site, as well as service work to restore the situation.

At the same time, a reduction of the charging power must be ensured if the energy supplier has to protect its grid in the event of undersupply.

In addition to overload protection, load management usually takes on further functions:

- Overload protection of the installation

- Optimization of the energy distribution to active charging sessions within the site

- Consideration of other consumers in the system and prioritization of these consumers over the charging infrastructure

- Prioritization of the charging groups among themselves

- Prioritization of the tariff used to charge the vehicles (e.g. grid-power or solar-optimized)


You can find more details under "Load management description" in the respective topic section.

### Billing e-mobility to tenants

Charging stations are large consumers and accordingly generate costs when used.
Primarily the energy used for charging, but also the operating power and the resulting losses, as well as any rental fee for the charging station.

Billing can be based on a single tariff or multiple tariffs, e.g. peak, off-peak and solar tariff, depending on the hardware used and the combination of billing solutions.

You can find more details under "Billing" in the respective topic section.

### Billing e-mobility to third parties or unknown users (public / semi-public)

Installing charging infrastructure often involves high costs. A good way to accelerate the payback or to enable a desired public offering is to make the charging station accessible to third parties. 

To make a charging station available to the public, e-mobility backend software is required.

The backend software then handles the authorization of charging for third parties depending on your needs and the selected payment method.

You can find more details under "Billing" in the respective topic section.
