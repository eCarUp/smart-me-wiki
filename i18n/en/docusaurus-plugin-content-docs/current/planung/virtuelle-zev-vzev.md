## The virtual association for own consumption – vZEV

A vZEV (virtual ZEV) is an association for own consumption that includes the grid. In a conventional ZEV (association for own consumption), all buildings must share a single grid connection. In a vZEV, you can combine several grid connections on the same low-voltage transformer (&lt; 1kV).

Besides this basic requirement, other points must be clarified to find out whether a vZEV is possible in the neighbourhood and with whom.

These include:

- the actual grid topology

- connection types (cable-joint networks or distribution cabinets)

### Step 1: Initial vZEV assessment

You request this initial assessment of these topics directly from the distribution grid operator (DSO).

The assessment shows whether a vZEV is possible and, if so, with which participants.

The DSO must provide a positive or negative response with reasons within 14 days at the latest.

### Step 2: Identify participants and apply for the vZEV

<div className="row">
<div className="col col--7">

In the second step, you need to find out who is interested. Notify the potential participants that they can take part in the vZEV.
Record both the information and the positive responses in writing, with signatures confirming participation.
You will need these later to authorize the data transfer and as attachments to contracts and other documents.

At this point, you must submit an official vZEV registration to the DSO that lists all interested parties. The DSO then checks and settles further points about the vZEV within 3 months.

- Production capacity must be at least 10% of the consumption capacity.

- Are all participants behind the same connection point at low-voltage level?

- Are smart meters already installed at all sites? (If not, they must be retrofitted.)

- Is other hardware available to read the metering points locally, and/or is there an application for SDTA transmission via Swisseldex?

Once all the points above have been met, after about 3 months, the vZEV can start operating.

### Multi-building vZEV (extended vZEV)

<div className="row">
<div className="col col--7">

Several ZEVs or buildings with their own grid connection share the same low-voltage connection (&lt;1kV)

They may now use the connecting lines between the buildings to transmit solar power.

This makes the energy produced in the buildings on the left also available to the multi-family buildings on the right.

It is not available to the single-family house, which lies outside the approved topology of a vZEV.

The topology requirements for taking part in the vZEV are described in more detail below.

## Possible members of a vZEV and the metering technology required

In a vZEV, different infrastructures from the grid operator and from private parties can come together. Metering for the various buildings can be provided in different ways and is combined into a single tariff model in the vZEV.

- Former practice model (DSO)

- ZEV with private metering (balancing meter from the DSO)

- Single-family houses with solar production

- Individual apartments and houses without solar production, with private metering or DSO metering.

## Meter infrastructure for the smart-me vZEV tariff model

Tariffs in a vZEV are based on equal rights for all participants. Every participant has the same claim to the solar power currently being produced.

For the tariffs of the different models to work together, you need selected meter units that are stored in our tariff as a reference.

The figure below shows all metering points needed for correct balancing and tariffs for each infrastructure.

![Virtual ZEV (vZEV) – Figure 9](/img/planung-virtuelle-zev-vzev/09.png)

### Former practice model (privatized former DSO model converted to a vZEV)

In this model, there is no metering point acting as a balancing meter, neither from the energy supplier nor a private one. This ZEV is therefore balanced virtually.
This requires metering of all producers and all consumers. The model works as a single building and also as part of an extended vZEV.

### Private ZEV (with balancing meter)

In this model, there is at least one metering point of the energy supplier at the main connection point of the ZEV. The data from this metering point can be imported into smart-me either through private sub-metering or through the energy supplier's data hub.

Private sub-metering allows efficient real-time control of the ZEV, which the energy supplier's meter can only support to a limited extent.

[More about the metering concept of private ZEVs](/planung/zev-zusammenschluss-zum-eigenverbrauch)

### House with solar production (single-family house)

In this model, there is at least one metering point of the energy supplier at the main connection point and, in some cases, also a production meter.
Tariffs for a terraced house are possible without a solar production meter. Ideally, however, the solar production of the terraced house is also metered, either by the DSO or privately.
This data can be collected through private metering with a smart-me Telstar or as a data hub import via Swisseldex.

### House or apartment without solar production (single-family house)

In this model, there is at least one metering point of the energy supplier at the building connection. The data of these consumers can be collected as a data hub import via Swisseldex.

A multi-family building without a solar system but with private metering follows the metering infrastructure of a ZEV without production.

## Importing metering data for energy supplier metering points

The metering data of the relevant metering points can be imported via the Swisseldex data hub.
smart-me is listed with Swisseldex as a recipient of this data 

After a request and authorization, the energy suppliers send the data via Swisseldex to the smart-me import data hub. The data can then be managed, visualized and billed in the respective smart-me account.

Characteristics of the energy suppliers' data:

- EBIX, SDAT data format

- 15-minute resolution

- Provided approx. every 24h, unverified

- Provided approx. every 30 days, verified

You can find detailed instructions on importing data here: [Details on the Swisseldex import](/schnittstellen/swisseldex-sdat-import)

## FAQ

<details id="kann-ein-vzev-in-einem-muffennetz-entstehen">
<summary>Can a vZEV be set up in a cable-joint network?</summary>

Yes, it is possible, but the requirements are very rarely met.

For a vZEV to be set up in a cable-joint network, all buildings must share exactly the same cable joint. Otherwise, the public grid between one joint and the next is included, which no longer meets the vZEV regulations.

</details>

<details id="kann-ein-gebäude-ohne-solaranlage-einem-vzev-beitreten-und-die-vnb-zähler-durch-privatzähler-ersetzen">
<summary>Can a building without a solar system join a vZEV and replace the DSO meters with private meters?</summary>

Yes, for the in-house production and consumption metering.
Within an established vZEV, the vZEV operator is responsible for metering and billing. This also means that the vZEV operator can use the metering equipment of their choice for the entire vZEV.

However, the distribution grid operator (DSO) of course also needs metering points for its own check metering and balancing of the vZEV. The DSO must set these accordingly.

If a multi-family building without a solar system wants to join the vZEV, a private meter can be installed for each billing unit. The DSO must then set a metering point at the building connection.

</details>

<details id="ist-eine-verschachtelung-von-mehreren-zev-in-einer-vzev-möglich">
<summary>Can several ZEVs be nested within one vZEV?</summary>

Yes and no.

You can form a vZEV from two existing ZEVs on the same transformer connection.
However, the two ZEVs cannot remain legally separate.
Legally, these two ZEVs become a single (v)ZEV.

The two ZEVs are therefore legally dissolved and replaced by one (v)ZEV.

</details>

<details id="kann-die-interne-tarifierung-und-der-strompreis-des-zev-oder-vzevs-frei-gewählt-werden">
<summary>Can the internal tariffs and the electricity price of the ZEV or vZEV be chosen freely?</summary>

Yes and no.

The rules are exactly the same as for a ZEV.

The tariff structure within a ZEV or vZEV may be set under private law and may differ from what the distribution grid operator (DSO) offers.
However, the legal principles of the ZEV regarding pricing still apply.

Example:
If grid electricity is charged as a single tariff with a capacity tariff, the ZEV or vZEV may decide internally to offer only a single tariff or a dual tariff.
However, regardless of the tariff chosen, you must make sure that no more money is collected than the grid electricity actually cost.

Internal solar power must also follow the rules and, depending on the rule applied, may not cost more than 80% to a maximum of 100% of the equivalent grid electricity.

</details>
