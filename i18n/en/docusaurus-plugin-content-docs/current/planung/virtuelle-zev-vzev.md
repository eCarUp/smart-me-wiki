## The virtual association for own consumption - vZEV

A vZEV is an association for own consumption that includes the grid. Unlike a conventional ZEV (association for own consumption), where all buildings must share one and the same grid connection, a vZEV makes it possible to combine several grid connections on one and the same low-voltage transformer (&lt; 1kV).

Apart from this basic requirement, further points must be clarified to determine whether, and with whom in the neighbourhood, a vZEV can be set up.

These include:

- the actual grid topology

- connection types (sleeve-joint networks or distribution cabinets)

### Step 1: Initial vZEV clarification

This initial clarification of these topics is requested directly from the distribution grid operator (DSO).

Afterwards, it becomes clear whether a vZEV is possible and, if so, with which participants.

The DSO must provide this information, with a positive or negative response and the reasons for it, within 14 days at the latest.

### Step 2: Determine participants and apply for the vZEV

<div className="row">
<div className="col col--7">

In a second step, you need to find out who is interested. Notify the potential participants of the opportunity to take part in the vZEV.
Both this information and the positive responses should be recorded in writing, with signatures confirming participation.
These will be needed later for the data transfer authorization, as well as attachments to contracts and other documents.

At this point, an official vZEV registration listing all interested parties must be submitted to the DSO. Within 3 months, further points relating to the vZEV are checked and completed.

- Production capacity must be at least 10% of the consumption capacity.

- Are all participants located behind the same connection point at low-voltage level?

- Are smart meters already installed at all locations or not? (If not, they must be retrofitted)

- Is further hardware available for reading the metering points locally, and/or is there an application for SDTA transmission via Swisseldex?

Once all of the above points have been met and after around 3 months, the vZEV can start operating.

### Multi-building vZEV (extended vZEV)

<div className="row">
<div className="col col--7">

## Possible members of a vZEV and the metering technology required

In a vZEV, different infrastructures from the grid operator and from the private sector may come together. Metering for the various buildings can be provided in different ways and is combined in the vZEV into a uniform tariff model.

- Former practice model (DSO)

- ZEV with private metering (balancing meter from the DSO)

- Single-family houses with solar production

- Individual apartments and houses without solar production, with private metering or DSO metering.

## Meter infrastructure for the smart-me vZEV tariff model

Tariffing in a vZEV is based on equal rights for all participants. All participants have an equal claim to the solar power currently being produced.

For the tariffing of the different models to work together, selected meter units are required so that they can be stored as references in our tariff.

### Ex-practice model (privatized ex-DSO model converted to vZEV)

In this model, there is no metering point serving as a balancing meter, neither from the energy supplier nor private. This ZEV is therefore balanced virtually.
This requires metering of all producers and all consumers. The model works as a single building, but also as part of an extended vZEV.

### Private ZEV (with balancing meter)

In this model, there is at least one metering point of the energy supplier at the main ZEV connection point. The data from this metering point can be imported into smart-me either via private sub-metering or via the energy supplier's data hub.

Private sub-metering enables efficient real-time control of the ZEV, which is only possible to a limited extent with the energy supplier's meter.

[More about the metering concept of private ZEVs](/planung/zev-zusammenschluss-zum-eigenverbrauch)

### House with solar production (SFH)

In this model, there is at least one metering point of the energy supplier at the main connection point and, in some cases, also a production meter.
Tariffing of a terraced house is possible without a solar production meter. Ideally, however, the solar production of the terraced house is also measured, either by the DSO or privately.
This data can be collected through metering with a smart-me Telstar as private metering, or as a data hub import via Swisseldex.

### House or apartment without solar production (SFH)

In this model, there is at least one metering point of the energy supplier at the house connection. The data of these consumers can be collected as a data hub import via Swisseldex.

A multi-family house without a solar installation but with private metering follows the metering infrastructure of a ZEV without production.

## Metering data import for energy supplier metering points

The metering data of the relevant metering points can be imported via the Swisseldex data hub.
Smart-me is listed with Swisseldex as a recipient of this data. 

Upon request and authorization, the energy suppliers send the data via Swisseldex to the smart-me import data hub. The data can then be managed, visualized and billed in the respective smart-me account.

Properties of the energy suppliers' data:

- EBIX, SDAT data format

- 15-minute resolution

- Provided approx. every 24h, unverified

- Provided approx. every 30 days, verified

You can find detailed instructions for the data import here: [Details on the Swisseldex import](/schnittstellen/swisseldex-sdat-import)

## FAQ

<details id="kann-ein-vzev-in-einem-muffennetz-entstehen">
<summary>Can a vZEV be set up in a sleeve-joint network?</summary>

Yes, it is possible, but the requirements for it are only very rarely met.

For a vZEV to be set up in a sleeve-joint network, all buildings must share exactly the same sleeve joint. Otherwise, the public grid between one sleeve joint and the next is included, which no longer complies with vZEV regulations.

</details>

<details id="kann-ein-gebäude-ohne-solaranlage-einem-vzev-beitreten-und-die-vnb-zähler-durch-privatzähler-ersetzen">
<summary>Can a building without a solar installation join a vZEV and replace the DSO meters with private meters?</summary>

Yes, for the in-house production and consumption metering.
Within the established vZEV, the vZEV operator is independently responsible for metering and billing. This also implies that the vZEV operator can use the metering equipment of their choice for the entire vZEV.

However, the distribution grid operator naturally also needs metering points of its own for counter-metering and balancing the vZEV. It must set these accordingly.

If a multi-family house without a solar installation now wants to join the vZEV, a private meter can be installed for each billing unit. The distribution grid operator must then, however, set a metering point at the house connection accordingly.

</details>

<details id="ist-eine-verschachtelung-von-mehreren-zev-in-einer-vzev-möglich">
<summary>Is it possible to nest several ZEVs within one vZEV?</summary>

Yes and no.

It is possible to form a vZEV from two existing ZEVs on the same transformer connection.
However, it is not possible for the two ZEVs to continue to exist individually in legal terms.
Legally, these two ZEVs become a single (v)ZEV.

The two ZEVs are therefore legally dissolved and replaced by one (v)ZEV.

</details>

<details id="kann-die-interne-tarifierung-und-der-strompreis-des-zev-oder-vzevs-frei-gewählt-werden">
<summary>Can the internal tariffing and electricity price of the ZEV or vZEV be chosen freely?</summary>

Yes and no.

It is regulated in exactly the same way as for the ZEV.

The tariff structure within a ZEV or vZEV may be set independently under private law and may differ from the distribution grid operator's offering.
However, the legal principles of the ZEV regarding pricing continue to apply.

Example:
If grid electricity is charged as a single tariff with a demand tariff, it may also be decided within the ZEV or vZEV to offer only a single tariff or a dual tariff.
However, it must be ensured that, regardless of the tariffing chosen, no more money is collected than the grid electricity actually cost.

Internal solar power must also follow the rules and, depending on the rule applied, may not cost more than 80% to max. 100% of the equivalent grid electricity.

</details>
